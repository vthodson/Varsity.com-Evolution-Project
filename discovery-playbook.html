1
Audience segmentation & multi-audience experience
What we heard
The site treats every visitor the same, and misses the ones actively searching for help.

“The site serves many audiences — athletes, parents, coaches, spectators — but hasn't consistently delivered the best experience for each. Discovery content, like finding a camp or gym, is often missing because it isn't top of mind for teams creating product-specific content.”

What we'll do
Build entry points designed around what each audience actually needs.

Increase visibility across every business line and grow audience reach by designing around audience intent — so athletes, parents, coaches, and fans each land somewhere built for them.

See it
A homepage with audience-specific Explore cards (Cheer, Dance, Yearbook, Performing Arts, Fashion, Watch, Shop) and a global search bar — so coaches, parents, and fans each get their own way in instead of one generic homepage everyone has to dig through.

2
Content & CMS management
What we heard
Content is a hidden asset going stale, with no system built to balance its two jobs.

“Event content goes stale every year, there's no single owner for site-wide content strategy, and marketing needs a CMS they can update without developer support — even as that content is one of the site's most valuable, most underused assets. There's also no clear system for handling two very different jobs content has to do at once: keeping operationally critical information (dates, rules, logistics) accurate, while also building the discovery content that brings new audiences in. Right now, one tends to crowd out the other.”

What we'll do
Give operational content and growth content each a clear home — and a life far beyond the site.

Modernize the platform for scalable growth with stronger content governance and publishing workflows, backed by a self-service CMS and a content strategy that gives operational content and growth content each their own clear model — so keeping event logistics accurate doesn't come at the expense of building content that drives growth. The content already being created is real fuel for growth — modeled once and reused everywhere, the same event page, camp guide, or promo can flow straight into Varsity.com, social, email, BAND, SMS, and whatever channel comes next, instead of being rebuilt by hand for each one.

See it
A headless CMS built for non-developer publishing — with content types for both jobs, so day-to-day operational updates and growth-focused content each have a clear home, current and on-brand, ready to go to work well beyond the site the moment it's created.

3
Discoverability, SEO & AI-readiness
What we heard
Years of content sit unused because it isn't built for how people, or AI, search today.

“A large volume of content built up over years supports traditional SEO but isn't structured for AI-driven discovery — real value sitting idle, and one of the most significant current gaps.”

What we'll do
Turn the content library into the engine that drives organic growth.

Grow audience reach and organic demand by treating the content library as a growth engine, not an archive — structured, direct-answer content built for search and AI answer tools.

See it
A structured event page — clear sections for dates, rules, and actions — in the direct-answer format search engines and AI tools can actually parse and surface, instead of a wall of text that buries the value already there.

4
Lead capture & conversion tooling
What we heard
Forms create friction instead of connecting people to the right team fast.

“Contact and lead forms, and their routing, are a major pain point needing a fresh look. The goal is less information required before reaching the right team — landing pages need the same attention.”

What we'll do
Replace generic forms with smart, in-context next steps.

Grow conversion across the ecosystem by strengthening every handoff — measurable, with next-best-action prompts instead of generic forms.

See it
Event actions and Get event support replacing a generic contact form — visitors get routed straight to the right next step instead of filling out a form and waiting to hear back.

5
Ecommerce
What we heard
The current platform can't support commerce, and any fix needs to integrate simply with Shopify, not add enterprise weight.

“The current WordPress site wasn't built for e-commerce and is creating operational strain. Any new solution needs to be simple to integrate with Shopify — but not a heavy enterprise platform for a modest catalog.”

What we'll do
Bring Shopify's commerce into the site exactly where it belongs, and nowhere it doesn't.

Extend the commerce already running on Shopify into the Varsity.com experience — meeting the shopper where it makes sense. Quick, in-the-moment buys (event gear, tickets) stay in-line on the page; deeper shopping (uniforms, full team stores) routes to the dedicated store built for that job.

See it
On the event page, Event gear sits inline next to myVarsity and Tickets — a quick add, no detour. A full My Team Shop or catalog browse, on the other hand, hands off to Shopify's dedicated storefront, where the shopping itself is the point.

6
Analytics & measurement
What we heard
The data exists, but nothing is configured to actually put it to use.

“Most needed data should be available through GA4, but it isn't well configured — lead and SEO tracking both remain unresolved.”

What we'll do
Build measurement into the plan from day one, not as an afterthought.

Modernize the platform with dashboards built around agreed KPIs — defined before build starts, not bolted on after launch.

See it
The Analytics and Measurement workstream — KPIs defined before build starts, so tracking works on day one instead of being patched in after launch.

7
Build approach — internal vs. external, cost, timing
What we heard
There's real doubt about going it alone, and real pressure on timing and budget.

“There's uncertainty about executing this fully in-house, particularly for design and UX — alongside a preference to avoid templated, non-brand-aligned solutions, and timing tied to FY27 capital planning.”

What we'll do
Make every build decision — internal, external, timing — a deliberate one.

Establish program governance so decisions on scope, staffing, and partners get made on purpose — using internal resources and outside expertise where each is strongest.

See it
A timeline and governance plan — clear decision rights and a realistic pace, so scope and staffing decisions get made on purpose instead of by default.
